How to Set Up WhatsApp Cloud API (Step-by-Step, 2026)

You set up WhatsApp Cloud API by creating a free Meta developer app, adding the WhatsApp product to it, and using the test number Meta gives you — that alone lets you message up to five recipient numbers you register. To run it in production on your own number, you then add a permanent access token, connect your own phone number, and complete Meta's business verification, which requires real business documents.

What you need before you start

The list is short: a personal Facebook account, a few minutes to create a developer account, and — only for the production stage — a phone number for the business and documents that prove the business exists. Everything in the testing stage is free, and you do not need a credit card or a registered company to start testing. Keep one browser tab open for Meta for Developers and one for Meta Business Settings; you will move between the two.

Step 1 — Create a Meta developer account and an app

Go to developers.facebook.com and register as a developer with your Facebook account. Then choose My Apps → Create App. When Meta asks what you want to build, select the business option — this creates an app connected to a Meta business portfolio. Give the app a clear name (your business name works) and finish the wizard. An "app" here is not a mobile app; it is simply the container that holds your WhatsApp setup, its credentials and its settings.

Step 2 — Add the WhatsApp product

Inside your new app, find WhatsApp in the list of products and click Set up. Meta creates a test WhatsApp Business account for you automatically. On the API Setup page you will now see four things worth copying into a safe note: the test phone number Meta lends you, the Phone Number ID, the WhatsApp Business Account ID (often shortened to WABA ID), and a temporary access token. The token expires quickly — that is expected at this stage; the permanent one comes in Step 5.

Step 3 — Add recipients and send your first message

The test number can only message numbers you have added as recipients, and you can add up to five. On the same API Setup page, add your own mobile number as a recipient and confirm it with the code WhatsApp sends you. Then use the built-in Send and receive messages panel to send the ready-made "hello_world" template to your phone. If it arrives, your setup works end to end: app, account, number and permissions are all talking to each other. From here, developers usually connect a tool or a webhook to receive replies — for a no-code route, our guide to WhatsApp automation with n8n shows how the receiving side is normally built.

Step 4 — Understand the 24-hour messaging window

This rule shapes everything you build afterwards, so learn it now. When a customer messages your number, a 24-hour customer service window opens. Inside that window you can reply with free-form messages — normal text, images, documents. When the window closes, you can only start a conversation with a template message: a pre-written message that Meta has approved in advance, in categories such as utility (order updates, reminders), authentication (one-time codes) and marketing. Templates are submitted in your app under Message Templates, and each one is reviewed before you can use it. Plan your reminders and follow-ups around templates from the beginning, not as an afterthought.

Step 5 — Get a permanent token and connect your own number

Two upgrades turn the test setup into a production one. First, the permanent token: in Meta Business Settings, create a System User, assign your app and WhatsApp account to it, and generate a token with the WhatsApp messaging permission. Unlike the temporary token, this one does not expire, and it is what your automation will use every day. Second, your own number: back in the app's API Setup page, add a phone number for the business and verify it by SMS or voice call. A number that is actively used in the regular WhatsApp app needs to be migrated or replaced — many businesses simply use a separate number for the API, which is also cleaner for customers.

Treat the permanent token like a bank password. Anyone who has it can send messages as your business. Store it in your tool's credential vault or environment variables, never in a spreadsheet, a screenshot or a public document.

Going to production — business verification, honestly

Here is the step most tutorials skip, and the one that decides whether your setup can actually scale. Meta requires business verification for full production access — higher messaging limits, an official business profile, and the ability to message beyond small-scale testing. Verification happens in Business Settings under Security Centre, and Meta asks for documents in two areas: proof of the business's legal name, and proof of its address or phone number.

The document types Meta accepts for these slots are: a business registration or business licence, a tax document issued by a tax authority, a certificate of incorporation or articles of association, and a utility bill showing the business name and address. One detail matters enormously and is easy to get wrong: a bank statement is not accepted for the address slot, even though it may show your name. If your only address document is a bank statement, verification will stall — arrange one of the accepted document types in the business's own name before you start the process.

Until verification is complete, your WhatsApp Business account can sit in a restricted state: the test setup still works, but production messaging is limited and can fail with errors that simply mean "this business is not verified yet." That is a paperwork problem, not a technical one — no tool, kit or workaround removes it, and anyone who promises to bypass Meta verification is selling you a ban risk.

Honest limits — what to watch out for

  • Meta charges for conversations. The API itself has no licence fee, but Meta bills per conversation beyond a free monthly allowance, and rates differ by country and by conversation category. Testing on the test number is free; production is pay-as-you-go.
  • Templates must be approved before use. Write them carefully and submit them early — approval is usually fast, but a rejected template cannot be sent at all.
  • Verification can take time and documents get rejected. Mismatched names between your documents and your business portfolio are the most common cause. Make the names agree exactly before submitting.
  • Never use unofficial WhatsApp APIs. Services that connect through the personal WhatsApp app or scraped sessions violate Meta's terms and routinely get numbers banned permanently.
  • A number ban or a lapsed window is final. Build your follow-ups inside the rules — opted-in customers, approved templates, real value — rather than around them.

Frequently asked questions

Is WhatsApp Cloud API free to use?

Creating the app, testing with the test number and messaging your five registered recipients costs nothing. In production, Meta charges per conversation beyond a free monthly allowance, with prices that vary by country and category — so "free" is true for setup and testing, not for messaging at scale.

Why was my verification document rejected?

The usual reasons are that the document type is not on Meta's accepted list (a bank statement in the address slot is the classic case), the business name or address on the document does not match your business portfolio, or the scan is unreadable. Fix the mismatch, use an accepted document type, and resubmit — do not submit the same rejected document again.

Can I use my existing WhatsApp number?

Sometimes, through migration, but the simplest and most reliable route is a separate number for the API that is not active in the WhatsApp app. Whichever route you choose, do it before printing the number anywhere, because changing it later means re-verifying.
