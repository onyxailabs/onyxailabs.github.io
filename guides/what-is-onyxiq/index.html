What Is OnyxIQ? The YouTube Growth Studio, Explained

OnyxIQ is a YouTube Growth Studio — a browser extension and web app that helps creators check their channel and score a video's title, description and tags before publishing, using data from the channel itself. It is built by Onyx AI Labs, sold for a one-time payment rather than a monthly subscription, and designed so that every score it gives shows the reasoning behind it.

Who OnyxIQ is for

OnyxIQ is for creators who publish regularly and want a structured check before a video goes live: small and growing channels that cannot justify a monthly fee for a large analytics suite, faceless channels that compete on packaging and search, and anyone who has opened YouTube Studio, stared at a weak click-through rate, and wished they had caught the problem while the video was still a draft. It assumes no team and no analyst — it is a tool for the person who films, edits and uploads the video themselves.

The seven modules

1. Channel X-Ray

An audit of your own channel: how your recent videos perform, where the packaging is inconsistent, and which patterns repeat across your uploads. It gives you one place to see the channel the way a new viewer — and the recommendation system — encounters it.

2. Video X-Ray

A per-video breakdown of an individual upload: its title, description, tags and visible performance signals, laid out together so you can see why one video travelled and another stalled.

3. Keyword Lab

Keyword research drawn from real search behaviour, so topic ideas are checked against what people actually type into YouTube instead of what sounds good in a brainstorm.

4. Competitor Compare

A side-by-side comparison of your channel with real channels in your niche, so differences in topics, packaging and cadence are visible rather than guessed at.

5. Thumbnail Feed-Test

Your draft thumbnail shown inside a realistic feed, next to competing videos, at the size viewers actually see it — the test that reveals whether your text is readable and your image stands out before you commit to it.

6. AI Studio

A workspace for drafting and improving titles, descriptions and video ideas with AI assistance, working in English and Roman Urdu.

7. Upload Checklist

A final pre-publish checklist that pulls the earlier steps together, so the checks you ran during planning are confirmed at the moment it matters — just before the video goes public.

Where it runs

OnyxIQ comes in two forms that share the same modules: a Chrome extension, which works alongside YouTube and YouTube Studio while you are preparing an upload, and a web app, which opens in any modern browser when you want more room to plan. You can move between the two freely — the checks you run on a draft in the web app are the same checks waiting in the Upload Checklist when you publish through the extension.

Bring your own API key

OnyxIQ reads channel data through the official YouTube Data API. You can sign in with YouTube, or bring your own free YouTube Data API key (the "BYOK" model). Either way, the key and the data belong to you: processing happens in your browser, OnyxIQ keeps no server copy of your channel data, and there is no per-query meter charging you for your own numbers. The practical effect is that the tool's running cost does not grow with your usage, which is one reason it can be sold once instead of rented monthly.

Worked example — scoring a draft title (example)

The following is an illustrative example showing how the scoring is used; it is not a record of a real channel.

A creator is preparing a tutorial video and drafts the title "New Video — Testing an AI Tool". In Video X-Ray, OnyxIQ returns a low score for the package and breaks down why: the title contains no phrase a viewer would search for, it names no tool, and the description is a single line with no timestamps or supporting detail.

The creator revises the title to "How to Automate WhatsApp Replies with n8n (Free Setup)" — adding the searched task, the tool's name and a clear benefit — writes a fuller description with timestamps, and adds specific tags. The new package scores markedly higher, and just as importantly, the creator can see which changes moved the score. The score did not make the video good; it stopped a weak package from being published unnoticed.

Honest limits

OnyxIQ advises; it cannot guarantee views, subscribers or revenue, and no honest tool can. Its scores are a structured checklist built from visible factors — search phrasing, title clarity, description completeness, thumbnail readability — not access to YouTube's internal ranking systems, and a high score cannot rescue a video nobody wants to watch. What it can do is remove the avoidable mistakes: the unsearchable title, the empty description, the thumbnail that fails at small sizes. The rest — topic choice, the video itself, consistency — remains the creator's work.

Frequently asked questions

How much does OnyxIQ cost?

OnyxIQ Prime is a one-time payment of $49 — there is no subscription and no monthly fee. A free Basic version is also available, so you can try the core scoring workflow before paying anything.

Do I need my own YouTube API key for OnyxIQ?

Yes. OnyxIQ works on a bring-your-own-key model: you create a free YouTube Data API v3 key in your own Google Cloud project, and the tool runs entirely on your own quota. Your key and your channel data stay with you.

Does OnyxIQ work for small or brand-new channels?

Yes — arguably it helps small channels most, because every upload has to count. Scoring a title, description and tags before publishing, and checking real search demand first, prevents the zero-demand uploads that stall new channels. No tool can guarantee views, and OnyxIQ does not claim to.
